## 1. Two Kingdoms Rise — The "Founding" Phase

As Silla grew weak, regional warlords gained strength. Among them, **Gyeon Hwon (甄萱)** founded Later Baekje on the former Baekje lands, while **Gung Ye (弓裔)** established Later Goguryeo as the declared heir to the Goguryeo legacy. This was the moment the Korean peninsula fractured into three again.

In game terms, this is the **"founding" phase**. Who builds a stronger internal foundation and wins popular support faster will shape everything that follows. Beginners often rush into warfare here, but campaigns launched without an internal base don't last. ## 2. Gung Ye Falls, Goryeo Is Born — Popular Support Decides

The mighty Gung Ye gradually descended into tyranny and lost the hearts of his people. Eventually his ministers elevated his general **Wang Geon (王建)** as the new king, and Wang Geon renamed the kingdom **Goryeo**. A kingdom built on power collapsed when it lost its people — and the one who won the people's hearts opened a new era.

## 3. The Battle of Gochang — One Battle Shifts the Entire Diplomatic Map

Goryeo and Later Baekje were evenly matched for a long time. Then **Wang Geon won a decisive victory at the Battle of Gochang (present-day Andong)**, and the balance tilted firmly toward Goryeo. Regional warlords began defecting to Goryeo in succession, and the momentum shifted in one direction.

In game terms, this was a single decisive battle that **reshaped the entire network of alliances**. Victory here meant not just territorial gain — it changed who the surrounding factions chose to support. In strategy games, the outcome of a major battle ripples through the "relationship ratings" of nearby factions. This moment shows exactly why it matters to **concentrate your forces in a battle you can actually win**.

## 4. Later Baekje's Internal Conflict and Unification — The Lesson of Internal Cohesion

The powerful Later Baekje was brought down not from the outside, but **from within**. Gyeon Hwon's conflict with his son over succession grew so severe that Gyeon Hwon ultimately defected to Goryeo — one of the most dramatic turns in Korean medieval history. A Later Baekje riven by internal strife finally collapsed, and Wang Geon unified the Later Three Kingdoms.

The strongest faction, defeated not by an outside enemy but by internal division — this is the single most recurring lesson in strategic history. It's also the most common reason a beginner playing a strong faction ends up losing: **failing to manage internal affairs**.
