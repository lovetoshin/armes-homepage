Costco is fundamentally different from other retailers in one key way: **prices can swing dramatically depending on when a sale runs, and once an item is pulled, it may never come back.** That's why people say "go uninformed and you pay full price, go informed and you pay half." Weekly promotions rotate constantly, and it's all too common to arrive at the store and think, "Oh, this was on sale?" only after the fact.

The problem is that Costco doesn't publish a clean, comprehensive list of all discounted items. So savvy shoppers have developed their own methods to find out **what's cheap this week** before they ever leave home. This post covers how to get ahead of the weekly deals, how to read the signals hidden in Costco's price tags, and how to build habits that keep impulse spending in check.

## The Hidden Signals in Price Tags

Costco price tags carry signals that can save you money if you know how to read them. These alone can tell you whether an item is "buy it now before it's gone."

| Price Tag Signal | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price ends in **.97** | Marked-down (discounted) item | Now is the time to buy |
| **Asterisk (\*)** in upper right | No restock / possible discontinuation | Buy now if you need it |
| Price ends in **.99** | Regular price | No urgency |
| Price ends in **.00 / .88** etc. | Additional markdown or special event | Check the label for details |

The **.97 + asterisk** combination is a strong signal meaning "this is discounted and may disappear soon." For a deeper dive into price tag codes, see [The Hidden Meaning Behind Costco's Price Numbers and Asterisks](/blog/costco-price-tag-secrets).

## Smart Shopping Habits

### 1. Decide What You Need Before You Go
Costco's bulk, warehouse format makes impulse buying dangerously easy. Simply making a list before you head out can dramatically cut unnecessary spending.

### 2. Compare Unit Prices (per 100g or per item)
Don't assume "bulk = cheapest." When the same product comes in different sizes, you need to compare the **price per 100g or per unit** to find the true best deal. Get in the habit of checking the small unit-price figure on the tag. For comparing with online prices, check out [How to Find the Lowest Price Online](/blog/online-lowest-price).

### 3. Track Price Trends
Items you buy regularly go up and down in price. Knowing the trend lets you hold off when prices are high and stock up when they're low. For which categories tend to offer the best value at Costco, see [How to Pick the Best-Value Essentials at Costco](/blog/costco-best-value-items).

## Things to Watch Out For

- **Cheap doesn't always mean savings.** If you can't use it all before it goes bad, it's a loss no matter how low the unit price is. Think about your consumption rate first.
- **Don't forget the annual membership fee.** True savings only count when you factor in the cost of membership. See [Costco Membership: Shopping Habits That Make It Worth the Fee](/blog/costco-membership-worth) for guidance.
- **Asterisk items can trigger impulse buying.** The pressure of "this might disappear soon" can push you into buying things you don't actually need. Stay mindful.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. What's the fastest way to find out about this week's Costco deals?**
Registering items and receiving price alerts is the fastest method. Community tips come in quickly too, but the information is fragmented and easy to miss.

**Q. Does a price ending in .97 always mean it's on sale?**
Generally yes — it typically indicates a markdown. Regular prices usually end in .99. However, there may be exceptions depending on the location or timing, so check any additional label information.

**Q. What does the asterisk (\*) on a price tag mean?**
It signals that the item won't be restocked and may be discontinued. If you truly need it, buying when you see it is the safe move.

**Q. Doesn't bulk automatically mean it's cheaper?**
Not necessarily. You need to compare the price per 100g or per unit. A larger pack doesn't always mean a lower unit price.

**Q. Can I actually get my membership fee's worth?**
It depends on how often and how much you buy. Households that shop frequently and in large quantities can easily save more than the annual fee. Start by reviewing your shopping patterns.

**Q. Do I need to arrive early to get discounted items?**
For popular sales and discontinued (asterisk) items, stock can move fast. If an item is essential to you, visiting early in the sale period is a safer bet.

**Q. What if I bought something cheap but can't use it all?**
That's Costco's most common trap. A low unit price means nothing if you end up throwing half of it away. Always consider your consumption speed and storage capacity first.

**Q. Is Costco always cheaper than buying online?**
It depends on the item. The safest habit is to compare unit prices against online deals before committing.
