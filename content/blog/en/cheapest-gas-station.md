Within the same neighborhood, gas prices can vary by tens of won per liter — sometimes over 100 won. That might sound minor, but the math tells a different story. A driver who fills up twice a month, 50 liters each time, loses 10,000 won a month from a 100-won-per-liter difference — that's 120,000 won a year. Same fuel, just money silently wasted.

The problem is that "checking every time is too much effort." So most people just pull into the same old station out of habit. This post covers how to quickly find the cheapest station nearby — and how to save even more at the pump once you've found it.

## Where Can You Check Gas Prices?

Real-time gas prices at stations nationwide are **publicly available.** The most prominent source is **Opinet**, run by Korea's National Oil Corporation. Navigation apps and location-based lifestyle apps also show nearby stations ranked by distance and price. The key is being able to **compare prices at multiple nearby stations in one view**.

| Method | Features | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opinet (National Oil Corp.) | Official nationwide data | Accurate price comparison |
| Navigation apps | Shows stations along your route | While driving or on the move |
| Map apps | Nearby station locations | Quick local lookup |

## 5 Ways to Pay Even Less

Once you've found the cheapest nearby station, here are ways to save even more.

### 1. Use Self-Service Stations
No attendant labor cost means a lower price per liter. Why self-service is cheaper — and how to use discount gas cards alongside it — is covered in detail in [Why Self-Service Gas Is Cheaper + Gas Discount Card Roundup](/blog/self-gas-discount-card).

### 2. Brand and Location Matter More Than Day or Time
"Certain days are cheaper" is more myth than reality. In practice, **brand and location** affect prices far more. Even within the same brand, stations in lower-rent suburban areas tend to be cheaper.

### 3. Get a Gas Discount Card
Combining per-liter discounts and statement credits from co-branded cards lowers your effective price one step further. Having one card matched to your fueling habits is worth it.

### 4. Time Your Fill-Ups With Price Trends
Domestic gas prices follow international crude oil with a 1–2 week lag. When oil prices are rising, fill up early. When they're falling, there's no rush — wait a bit.

### 5. Collect Points and Rewards
If the price is the same, you might as well earn something. Telecom carrier, credit card, and gas station membership points add up over time and shouldn't be ignored.

One common confusion: premium fuel. **Putting premium in a car that doesn't need it is simply paying more for no benefit.** Check whether your car actually needs premium at [Premium vs. Regular Fuel: What Does Your Car Need?](/blog/premium-vs-regular-gas).

## Things to Keep in Mind

- **Going far out of your way to save negates the savings.** Driving 10 extra minutes to save 30 won per liter costs you more in fuel and time. "Cheapest among the stations near you" is the right answer.
- **Quality is largely the same.** Volume and quality are regulated, so self-service doesn't mean lower-quality fuel. Price-first is a reasonable approach.
- **Check card benefit conditions.** Many cards have minimum monthly spend requirements and discount caps — always read the terms.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Where can I find the most accurate gas prices?**
Opinet, run by Korea's National Oil Corporation, is the official source with the most accurate data. Many navigation and map apps pull from Opinet as well.

**Q. Are self-service stations really cheaper?**
Generally, yes. Without attendant labor costs, per-liter prices at self-service stations tend to be lower than at full-service ones.

**Q. What day of the week is the cheapest to fill up?**
Day of the week matters less than brand and location. "Specific days are always cheaper" is not a reliable rule.

**Q. Is it worth filling up completely when prices are low?**
If fuel prices are rising, filling up early can make sense. But think of it as pulling forward your next fill-up by a bit — not stockpiling long-term.

**Q. Should I drive far out of my way to find a cheap station?**
Not recommended. The extra fuel and time spent getting there can easily exceed what you save. Cheapest among nearby options is the smartest choice.

**Q. Should I get a gas discount card?**
If you fill up frequently, it's worth it. Just check the minimum spend requirements and discount caps to find a card that matches your actual usage.

**Q. Is self-service fuel lower quality?**
No. Volume and quality are regulated regardless of service type. It's perfectly fine to choose based on price.

**Q. Does premium fuel help my car?**
Unless your car specifically requires it, regular fuel is sufficient. Putting in premium when it's not needed is just paying more with no benefit.
