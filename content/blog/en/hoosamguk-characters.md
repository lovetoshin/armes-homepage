## Faction Playstyles for Beginners

In strategy simulation games, each faction typically excels in different areas. Mapping the three Later Three Kingdoms figures to that lens:

### Gyeon Hwon (Later Baekje) — Military Force
Strong armed forces are this faction's calling card. Well-suited to beginners who are confident in direct confrontations. However, just as Gyeon Hwon's dynasty was ultimately undone by **internal family conflict**, neglecting internal cohesion will weaken even the strongest army.

### Gung Ye (Later Goguryeo) — Early Dominance
You start with wide territory and strong momentum, but **managing popular support** is the central challenge. If you enjoy aggressive early expansion, this faction has a lot of appeal — but fail to keep your people and ministers on side, and history will repeat itself: a sudden, dramatic collapse.

### Wang Geon (Goryeo) — Diplomacy and Comeback
At first glance the weakest of the three, but **diplomacy and popular sentiment** are how this faction turns the table. If you prefer earning allies over winning battles — absorbing local warlords, forming coalitions, winning people over — this faction offers the deepest long-term gameplay.

## The Story Arc — How They Clashed

The factions' characters are borne out in how history actually unfolded:

1. As Silla weakened, Gyeon Hwon (Later Baekje) and Gung Ye (Later Goguryeo) each established their kingdoms.
2. Wang Geon starts as a general under Gung Ye and wins notable victories.
3. As Gung Ye becomes increasingly tyrannical, his ministers raise Wang Geon as the new king and the kingdom is renamed Goryeo.
4. Goryeo (Wang Geon) and Later Baekje (Gyeon Hwon) then fight it out; family strife within Later Baekje gives Wang Geon the decisive edge.
5. Wang Geon unifies the Later Three Kingdoms.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Which faction is best for a beginner?**
Gyeon Hwon (Later Baekje) is the most approachable — direct combat is straightforward to learn. If you want the depth of diplomacy and politics right away, Wang Geon (Goryeo) is also a strong recommendation.

**Q. Which faction is the strongest?**
Gung Ye (Later Goguryeo) has the strongest early-game momentum. But internal management failures can cause a rapid collapse, so raw power doesn't mean it's easy to play.

**Q. Why recommend Wang Geon if he starts weak?**
The appeal is the comeback — the joy of reversing your position through diplomacy and popular support. In real history, Wang Geon was the underdog who became the final victor.

**Q. How were the three leaders connected historically?**
Wang Geon was originally a general serving under Gung Ye. When Gung Ye turned tyrannical, his ministers elevated Wang Geon as king and renamed the kingdom Goryeo, which then fought Gyeon Hwon's Later Baekje.

**Q. Why did Gyeon Hwon lose despite being so strong?**
His military was formidable, but an internal succession dispute proved fatal. Internal division is the most common cause of defeat in strategy games for exactly this reason.

**Q. Why did Gung Ye fall?**
He was powerful, but tyranny drove away the people and his ministers. Once their loyalty was gone, no military strength could protect him.

**Q. Can I enjoy the game without knowing the history?**
Yes. Knowing the strengths and weaknesses of each faction is enough to get started. The characters and events become familiar naturally as you play.
