The same product: ten dollars at one store, fifteen at another. Price differences in online shopping are completely normal — and a 50% gap isn't rare at all. The problem is that checking multiple sites every time is tedious, and the "lowest price" you worked hard to find often turns out to be a trap once you add shipping fees or check the actual option you wanted.

To truly get the lowest price, you need to know two things: ① **How to compare by actual checkout amount, not the displayed price**, and ② **How to judge whether now is a good time to buy by tracking price history**. This article covers both.

## Why Prices Differ Across Sites

| Reason | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Different sellers | The same product is sold by multiple sellers at their own prices |
| Coupon & reward differences | Even if the listed price is the same, card discounts and reward points change the actual checkout amount |
| Shipping fees | A cheaper product can become more expensive once shipping is added |
| Bundle vs. single unit | Per-unit prices differ for single vs. bundled quantities |

That's why you need to compare by **the actual checkout amount — including shipping and discounts** — to find the true lowest price.

## 3 Ways to Find the Lowest Price

### 1. Price Comparison Sites & Apps
These aggregate prices for the same product from multiple shopping platforms on a single screen. The fastest way to get a feel for the going rate.

### 2. Portal Shopping Search
The shopping search on portals like Naver or Daum lists multiple sellers of the same product sorted by price.

### 3. Price History Tracking
To know whether the current price is actually cheap, you need to look at **past price trends**. Everyday items like household goods go up and down in price — track the history and you can catch the dip. Tracking specific retailers like Costco is covered separately in [How to Check Costco's Weekly Deals](/blog/costco-weekly-deals). ## How to Avoid Being Fooled by "Lowest Price" Claims

| Trap | Tactic | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bait lowest price | Product is cheap, but shipping fees make up the difference | Check total checkout including shipping |
| Option trap | The representative price is for the cheapest option, but the one you actually want is pricier | Select the option you want before comparing prices |
| Fake discount | Inflated original price makes the discount look huge | Compare to past prices and other sites |
| Unit confusion | Bundle vs. single unit pricing gets mixed up | Compare price per unit or per 100g |

Trust the **amount shown at the very last step before checkout**, not the displayed number.

## Things to Watch Out For

- **Don't waste time hunting for a tiny discount.** Spending an hour to save a few cents is a net loss. Focus on big-ticket purchases.
- **Be suspicious of prices that are way too cheap.** If a price is far below market rate, check for counterfeits, used items, or defects.
- **Check seller reputation.** Even at the lowest price, avoid sellers with poor reviews and ratings.

## Conclusion

The key to finding the lowest price online is **comparing by actual checkout amount rather than the listed price**, and **timing your purchase by watching price trends**. Use price comparison tools to get the market rate, factor in shipping fees and discounts, and check past price history and seller credibility — and you'll be able to buy the same product at the lowest price, safely.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Can I just trust price comparison sites?**
They're great for getting a sense of the market rate, but coupons, card discounts, and shipping fees may not be reflected. Always double-check the final amount before checkout.

**Q. Isn't the listed lowest price actually the cheapest?**
Not necessarily. Adding shipping fees, checking the right option, and applying or removing coupons can change the ranking. Compare by actual checkout amount.

**Q. How do I know if now is a good time to buy?**
You need to look at past price history. For items you buy regularly, setting up price tracking alerts lets you catch the dip without searching every day.

**Q. Are products with a large discount percentage the best deal?**
Sometimes the original price is inflated to make the discount look bigger. Focus on the actual price compared to other stores, not the discount percentage.

**Q. Why should I be suspicious of an unusually low price?**
It could be a counterfeit, a used item, a defective product, or a bait-and-switch. If the price is far below market rate, check the seller and product condition carefully.

**Q. How do I avoid the option trap?**
Don't use the representative price — select the exact option you intend to buy and compare that price. The representative price is usually the cheapest option.

**Q. Which is cheaper — a bundle or individual units?**
Calculate and compare the per-unit or per-100g price. Bundles aren't always cheaper.

**Q. How do I use price alerts effectively?**
Register the products you're watching and set a target price — you'll get notified when it drops below that threshold. Saves you from searching every day.
