## Charging Types First

EV charging comes in two main types. Understanding this distinction covers half of what you need to know.

| Type | Level 2 (AC) | DC Fast Charging |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 4–10 hours | ~30 minutes (to 80%) |
| Cost | Lower | Higher |
| Battery | Gentle (better for lifespan) | Some stress |
| Used at | Home, workplace, destination | Highway stops, on-the-go |

- **Level 2 Charging:** Takes several hours but costs less and is gentle on the battery. Best for places where you'll be parked for a while — home, work, your destination.
- **DC Fast Charging:** Gets to 80% in about 30 minutes. Ideal for a quick top-up on a trip, but costs more.

For the differences between these types and how to find stations, see [Finding EV Charging Stations — DC Fast vs. Level 2](/blog/ev-charger-finder).

## Step-by-Step Charging Process (DC Fast Charger)

The process is simpler than it looks the first time.

1. Park at the charging station and turn off the car.
2. On the charger's screen, tap Start, then authenticate with your membership card or app.
3. Insert the connector into your car's charging port — **push it all the way in until it clicks.**
4. Once charging starts, you can wait in the car.
5. When done, remove the connector and hang it back on the charger.

Do it once and it'll feel simpler than pumping gas from your second time onward.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

```text
[Mistake 1] Parking on the wrong side
  → Cable doesn't reach — have to re-park ❌
[Fix] Before parking, orient the car so your charging port faces the charger

[Mistake 2] Trying to DC fast charge to 100%
  → Charging slows to a crawl above 80% — wasted time and money ❌
[Fix] Stop DC fast charging at 80%

[Mistake 3] Not inserting the connector fully
  → Charging doesn't start ❌
[Fix] Push until you hear/feel a click
```

## Essential Basics to Know

| Tip | Details |
| --- | --- |
| The 80% rule | DC fast charging slows dramatically above 80% — stop there |
| Level 2 first | Better for battery life and cheaper |
| Charging port direction | Park so your port faces the charger — cables have limited reach |
| Winter range | Cold weather reduces range — this is normal |

### Why Charging Slows After 80%
The battery management system deliberately slows DC fast charging above 80% to protect battery health. Fast charging to 80% and freeing up the spot is more efficient for you and more considerate to the driver behind you.

### Level 2 as Your Default
Level 2 is better for both battery longevity and cost. The right habit: **Level 2 for everyday charging, DC fast only when you need it.** For cost-saving strategies, see [EV Charging Costs: Time-of-Use and Membership Savings](/blog/ev-charging-cost-saving).

### Charging Port Direction and Cable Length
Before you pull into a spot, check which side your charging port is on and make sure it faces the charger. First-timers often miss this and have to pull out and re-park.

## Conclusion

EV charging comes down to three principles: **Level 2 as your default, stop DC fast charging at 80%, and check availability before you go.** As long as you point your charging port toward the charger and push the connector all the way in, your first charge won't be stressful. Once you get past that initial unfamiliarity, charging will quickly feel easier and more convenient than a gas station.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Do I need to charge every day?**
No. Depending on your driving habits, every few days is usually enough. With Level 2 at home or work, you'll barely notice charging as part of your routine.

**Q. How full should I charge?**
For daily driving, Level 2 to 80–90% is fine. Stop DC fast charging at 80%. There's no need to reach 100% every time.

**Q. Should I drain the battery to 0% before charging?**
No — avoid deep discharges. Charge when you're around 20–30% remaining. Think of it like a smartphone: don't let it die completely.

**Q. Is DC fast charging bad for the battery if I use it all the time?**
It does put more stress on the battery than Level 2. Mixing in regular Level 2 charging while using DC fast only when needed is better for long-term battery health.

**Q. I plugged in but charging won't start. What's wrong?**
Usually the connector isn't fully inserted, or the authentication step wasn't completed. Push the connector until you feel/hear a click, and confirm that payment or card authentication went through.

**Q. My winter range is shorter than the spec says. Is something wrong?**
Perfectly normal. Cold temperatures reduce battery efficiency, which shortens driving range. Pre-conditioning the cabin while still plugged in (using the car's scheduled heating) helps offset this.

**Q. Can I own an EV without a home charger?**
Yes. Workplace chargers, apartment building shared chargers, and destination Level 2 charging at grocery stores and parking lots are all viable options. The key is knowing what's available in your daily environment before you need it.

**Q. How does payment work at a charging station?**
Authentication and payment are typically handled via the provider's membership card or app. Joining the membership for providers that are common in your area also unlocks discounted rates.
