## Why Self-Service Stations Are Cheaper

There are concrete reasons why self-service stations consistently undercut full-service stations on price.

| Reason | Explanation |
| --- | --- |
| No labor cost | No pump attendants = lower operating cost, passed on in price |
| High-volume, thin-margin model | Higher throughput allows lower per-liter pricing |
| You do the work | A small effort on your end = savings of tens of won per liter |

The trade-off is minimal — you pump your own gas — and the per-liter savings are real every single time. That said, prices vary even among self-service stations depending on location and brand, so finding the cheapest one nearby still matters. ## How to Choose a Fuel Discount Card

There are a lot of options, and it can feel overwhelming. Check these four things before deciding.

| What to Check | The Key Question |
| --- | --- |
| Discount type | Per-liter discount or statement credit? |
| Spending requirement | Can you realistically hit the monthly threshold? |
| Monthly cap | What's the maximum discount per month? |
| Annual fee | Does total annual discount minus annual fee stay positive? |

### 1. Per-Liter Discount vs. Statement Credit
Some cards offer a flat discount per liter — like "60 won off per liter." Others apply a percentage rebate to your total bill. **If you fill up frequently, a per-liter discount tends to be more valuable.**

### 2. Monthly Spending Requirement
Most cards require you to spend a minimum amount the prior month before the discount kicks in. Make sure your regular spending already meets that threshold — otherwise, the discount is irrelevant.

### 3. Monthly Discount Cap
Cards usually cap the monthly discount — something like "up to 10,000 won per month." If you fill up a lot, look for cards with a higher cap.

### 4. Annual Fee
If the annual fee exceeds the total annual discount, you're losing money. Always calculate: **total annual discount − annual fee**. If it's not positive, that card isn't worth it for you.

## Stacking Additional Savings

- **Carrier and membership rewards:** Some stations offer extra points through telecom or loyalty partnerships.
- **Redeem points at the pump:** Points you've accumulated can be used like cash toward gas.
- **App coupons:** Fuel-related apps sometimes offer limited-time discount coupons — keep an eye out.

Add a timing strategy — fill up when prices are trending up, hold off when they're falling — and the savings compound further. Not sure whether to use premium or regular? See [Premium vs. Regular Gas — Which Fuel Is Right for Your Car?](/blog/premium-vs-regular-gas).

## Things to Watch Out For

- **Don't pile up cards just for fuel discounts.** Managing multiple annual fees and spending thresholds becomes its own burden. One card that fits your habits is enough.
- **Check if benefits have changed.** Card terms can be updated. Review your card's discount structure periodically.
- **Self-service quality is the same.** Quantity and quality are regulated — self-service fuel is no different from full-service. Choose by price, not fear.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Why are self-service stations cheaper?**
No pump attendants means lower labor costs, which are reflected in the price. They also rely on high volume and thin margins to compete.

**Q. Is self-service fuel lower quality?**
No. Quantity and quality are both regulated — self-service fuel is the same as full-service. You can choose based entirely on price.

**Q. Which is better — per-liter discount or statement credit?**
If you fill up frequently, per-liter is usually better. If most of your spending is on non-fuel purchases, a statement credit card may win out. Run the math on your own habits.

**Q. Do I have to meet the monthly spending requirement?**
Most discounts require it. If you can't hit the threshold with your regular spending, the discount won't apply — so always check before you apply.

**Q. Is the annual fee worth it?**
Calculate total annual discount minus annual fee. If the result isn't positive for you, that card isn't worth having.

**Q. How many fuel discount cards should I have?**
One that matches your usage pattern is ideal. Stacking cards just adds complexity with annual fees and spending minimums to track.

**Q. Can I use points toward gas?**
In many cases, yes. Telecom carrier points, membership rewards, and card points can often be used at the pump like cash. Use them.

**Q. What's the most money-saving combination overall?**
Fill up at the cheapest self-service station near you, with a discount card matched to your spending pattern, and redeem any available points on top.
