These days, every company talks about "AI." But ARMES didn't start from flashy technology. It started from **the frustrations of the real world.** Not grand vision, but a very specific feeling of helplessness — "Couldn't someone just handle this repetitive work for me?" came first.

ARMES's founder spent a long time in the distribution industry. Selling products, meeting customers, doing the same things day after day. Through that experience, one thing became clear: **the time needed for things that actually matter kept getting eaten up by repetitive tasks.** This piece shares how those frustrations became real services, and the principles ARMES works by.

## 3 Things We Care About Most

| Principle | What It Means |
| --- | --- |
| Technology used every day | Not demos — tools that actually run in the field |
| Quality that keeps people | A user disappointed by their first experience won't come back |
| Verify, then expand | Take capabilities proven in one area into the next |

The second point — **quality** — is a philosophy earned from 16 years in distribution. If the first screen falls short, users won't give you a second chance. So ARMES operates by the principle of getting it right from the start, not "we'll fix it later."

## Why We Don't Stop at Shopping

The journey began with a tool to help sellers (SellerAI), but the frustration of repetition wasn't limited to shopping. Comparing travel deals every time (TravelMoa), searching for local benefits one by one (RewardTalk), tracking Costco prices (CocoPing) — these are all the same kind of friction. So ARMES has been **taking capabilities proven in one area and expanding them into everyday life at large.** The free conversion and utility tools are part of the same thread — [Developer Online Tool Collection](/blog/dev-tools) is one example.

## Conclusion

ARMES's reason for doing AI is simple. We believe that **technology only becomes meaningful when it solves real-life problems.** Not impressive demos, but tools people use every day. Not adequate quality, but quality good enough that people stay — that belief is what we're turning into services, one by one. Because we started from the frustrations of the real world, ARMES's next service will always begin from "someone's repetitive day."

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. What kind of company is ARMES?**
A company building real-life services that reduce the repetitive tasks of distribution and daily life using AI technology. We build tools across shopping, travel, and everyday life.

**Q. Why do you call yourself a "service company" rather than an "AI technology company"?**
Because technology isn't the goal — solving real problems people face is. AI is the tool we use to solve those problems.

**Q. What services are you building?**
SellerAI for sellers, TravelMoa for travel deals, RewardTalk for local benefits, and CocoPing for Costco shopping, among others.

**Q. What's the most important value you hold?**
Quality. The lesson from the distribution industry is that a user disappointed by their first experience doesn't come back — and we apply that lesson directly to our products.

**Q. Can non-sellers use your products?**
Yes. The travel, lifestyle, and shopping tools are for general users. We also provide utility tools like free conversion and organizational tools that anyone can use.

**Q. Why do you build multiple services instead of focusing on one area?**
Because "the frustration of repetition" doesn't live in just one area. We take capabilities proven in one place and expand them into other everyday frustrations.

**Q. What direction are you heading in?**
Same as always — starting from "someone's repetitive day." We keep building services that make real life a little lighter.
