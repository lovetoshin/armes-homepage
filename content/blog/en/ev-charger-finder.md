For new EV owners, charging is the most intimidating part of switching. Gas stations line every major road, so running low on fuel never felt like a crisis — but charging stations are harder to find, and when you do find one, the spot might be taken or the charger might be broken. On top of that, charging speed and pricing vary by station and provider, which means "where do I go to charge fast and cheap?" is a constant question.

Most of that anxiety disappears once you get into the habit of **finding stations in advance and checking availability, pricing, and charger status before you leave.** This guide covers the difference between DC fast and Level 2 charging, how to find charging stations quickly, what to look for in a charging app, and practical tips for reducing your charging costs.

## DC Fast Charging vs. Level 2 — What's the Difference?

This is the essential first thing to understand. EV charging comes in two main types.

| Type | DC Fast Charging | Level 2 Charging |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time | ~30 minutes (to 80%) | 4–10 hours |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |
| Battery impact | Some stress | Gentle (battery-friendly) |
| Best for | Highway stops, on-the-go | Home, workplace, destination |

- **DC Fast Charging:** Gets you to 80% in about 30 minutes. Great for topping up during a trip, but costs more and puts more stress on the battery.
- **Level 2 Charging:** Takes several hours, but costs less and is gentler on the battery. Best suited for places where you'll be parked for a while — home, work, or your destination.

The most economical approach: **Level 2 as your default, DC fast only when you need it.** For a detailed cost comparison, see [EV Charging Costs: Time-of-Use and Membership Savings](/blog/ev-charging-cost-saving).

## How to Find Charging Stations

There are three main approaches.

| Method | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dedicated EV charging app | Shows real-time availability and fault status | Data quality varies by app |
| Map apps | Familiar and integrated with navigation | Weak on real-time status |
| Provider apps | Member discounts at that provider's stations | Limited to one provider's network |

Apps built on public government EV data are available, and general map and lifestyle apps also show nearby stations. A good app shows you all of this at a glance:

- Current **availability** (open spots)
- Charger **type and output (kW)**
- **Pricing** and operating hours
- **Out-of-service / maintenance** status

## Checklist for Choosing a Charging App

Different apps have different strengths. These are the things to check:

1. **Real-time availability:** Prevents the scenario of arriving to find no open spots. This is the most important feature.
2. **Fault / maintenance info:** Saves you from driving to a broken charger.
3. **Price comparison:** Rates differ between providers even in the same area.
4. **Favorites and route planning:** Lets you track your regular stations and plan charging stops for long trips.

## How to Save on Charging Costs

### 1. Charge During Off-Peak Hours
Electricity rates are lower during low-demand periods (typically overnight). If you have access to Level 2 charging at home or work, overnight charging is the most economical option.

### 2. Membership Cards
Sign up for memberships with the charging providers that are most common in your area. Member rates are typically lower than pay-as-you-go.

### 3. Destination Charging
Charge on Level 2 while you're already parked at a grocery store or shopping mall. You save time and money simultaneously.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Should I normally use DC fast or Level 2?**
Level 2 for everyday use — it's cheaper and gentler on the battery. DC fast is for when you're on the road and need a quick top-up.

**Q. What if I arrive at a station and all the spots are taken?**
Use an app that shows real-time availability before you leave. It's also worth having a backup station in mind.

**Q. Why is DC fast charging only recommended to 80%?**
The battery management system slows charging speed significantly above 80% to protect the battery. Going beyond 80% on a fast charger is inefficient and creates longer waits for other drivers — stop at 80% and move on.

**Q. What should I look for in a charging app?**
Real-time availability, fault/maintenance status, and price comparison. Those three features alone will prevent most wasted trips.

**Q. Is higher output (kW) always better?**
Higher kW means faster charging — but only up to your vehicle's maximum accept rate. Knowing your car's charging ceiling matters more than the station's advertised output.

**Q. How do I avoid broken chargers?**
Use an app that tracks out-of-service status. If your app doesn't show that, call the provider's customer support to verify before you head out.

**Q. How do I plan charging on a long road trip?**
Bookmark fast chargers along your route (highway rest stops are reliable). Fill to 80% at each stop and continue to the next — it's faster than going beyond 80%.

**Q. Is destination charging at a grocery store really worth it?**
Absolutely. You charge cheaply on Level 2 while you're already there anyway, and you collect the store's rewards or discounts at the same time — time, cost, and perks in one stop.
