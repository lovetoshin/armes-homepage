One of the biggest draws of going electric is **lower running costs.** Cover the same distance and you'll spend far less on electricity than you would on gas. But that advantage shrinks fast if you charge whenever and wherever with DC fast chargers. In practice, **when, where, and with which membership** you charge can make a two-to-one difference in what you pay for the same amount of energy.

To truly enjoy the cost advantage of an EV, you need to understand what determines your charging rate and **make the cheapest combination a habit.** This guide covers the factors that drive charging costs, how to use off-peak hours to your advantage, how to choose a membership, and how to build a destination charging habit — all with a practical focus.

## What Determines Your Charging Cost?

Four factors multiply together to set your charging cost. Each one has a real impact.

| Factor | Cost Impact | How to Save |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DC Fast vs. Level 2 | DC fast costs more per kWh | Use Level 2 as your default |
| Charging provider | Rates vary by provider | Find the cheapest in your area |
| Time of day | Off-peak hours are cheaper | Charge overnight / off-peak |
| Membership tier | Member discounts apply | Join the providers you use most |

- **DC Fast vs. Level 2:** Fast charging is faster but costs more per kWh. See [Finding EV Charging Stations — DC Fast vs. Level 2](/blog/ev-charger-finder) for a full comparison.
- **Charging provider:** Base rates differ between providers.
- **Time of day:** Electricity is cheaper during low-demand periods, and some providers pass those savings through.
- **Membership tier:** Members and non-members pay different rates; membership tier can affect the discount further.

## Using Off-Peak Hours

Electricity rates are lower during **off-peak hours** — typically overnight — when demand is low. If you have access to Level 2 charging at home or work, slow overnight charging is the most economical option you have.

| Charging Combination | Relative Cost | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 2 + off-peak | Cheapest | ★★★ |
| Level 2 + daytime | Moderate | ★★ |
| DC Fast + off-peak | Somewhat expensive | ★ |
| DC Fast + daytime | Most expensive | Emergency only |

- **Level 2 + off-peak:** The lowest-cost combination. Charge while you're parked for the night or the workday anyway.
- **DC Fast when necessary:** Reserve it for trips when you genuinely need a quick top-up.

Following the simple rule — **Level 2 by default, DC fast only when needed** — makes a noticeable dent in your monthly charging bill.

## How to Choose a Membership

Signing up with a charging provider's membership unlocks discounted rates. Here's what to evaluate:

### 1. Which Providers Are Near You?
High discount rates mean nothing if that provider's chargers aren't where you actually drive. Check which providers have chargers near your home and workplace first.

### 2. Discount Size and Conditions
Is the discount automatic upon joining, or does it require a monthly fee or usage threshold? If there's a monthly fee, calculate whether your typical charging volume covers it.

### 3. App Convenience
A good app that shows real-time availability and pricing saves you wasted trips. Usability matters as much as the discount rate.

## Monthly Cost Comparison (Example)

The same driving distance can produce very different monthly bills depending on your habits.

```text
[Driver A] DC fast, daytime, no membership
  Mostly fast charging, during the day, no member discount
  → Monthly charging bill: ~$65 (estimated)

[Driver B] Level 2, off-peak, membership
  Level 2 at home/work overnight + member discount
  DC fast only for long trips
  → Monthly charging bill: ~$30–35 (estimated)

Same mileage — roughly $30 difference per month from habits alone
```
*Actual amounts vary by vehicle, region, and provider rates. This illustrates the direction of the difference.*

## Conclusion

The core strategy for keeping EV charging costs low is **defaulting to Level 2 charging during off-peak hours and using your local memberships.** Save DC fast charging for when it's truly necessary, and build the habit of charging at your destinations. Add store rewards and discounts on top of that, and a single charging stop delivers twice the value. Small consistent habits are what turn an EV's low running cost advantage into a real, felt difference every month.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. What has the biggest impact on charging costs?**
Whether you use DC fast or Level 2, and what time of day you charge, make the biggest difference. The same amount of energy can cost nearly twice as much with "DC fast during daytime" versus "Level 2 off-peak."

**Q. Does charging off-peak really save money?**
Yes — for Level 2 at home or work, overnight charging takes advantage of lower electricity rates. DC fast chargers benefit less from time-of-use pricing.

**Q. Can I sign up for multiple provider memberships?**
Yes. But it's easier to manage if you focus on 1–2 providers that have the most chargers in the areas you actually use.

**Q. Is a monthly membership fee worth it?**
Calculate whether the discount savings on your typical monthly charging volume exceed the fee. If you charge infrequently, a fee-free membership might be better.

**Q. Is DC fast charging always a bad choice?**
No — for long trips and urgent situations, it's necessary. The practical rule is "Level 2 by default, DC fast when needed."

**Q. What if I don't have home charging access?**
You can still save. Workplace chargers, apartment shared Level 2, and destination charging at grocery stores and parking lots all count. Finding Level 2 options in your daily routine is the key.

**Q. Is destination charging really worth it?**
You save on charging costs by using cheap Level 2 while you're already there, and you pick up store rewards or discounts at the same time — time, money, and perks in one stop. It's the most effective cost-saving habit.

**Q. Are EVs really cheaper to run than gas cars?**
With good charging habits, definitely. But if you rely mostly on DC fast charging during peak hours, that gap shrinks. The Level 2 + off-peak + membership combination is what makes the advantage real.
