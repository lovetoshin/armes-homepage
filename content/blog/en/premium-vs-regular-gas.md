## The Real Difference Between Premium and Regular

The key concept is the **octane rating**. Octane measures how well a fuel resists abnormal combustion inside the engine — a phenomenon known as engine knock. A higher octane rating doesn't mean "more power"; it means **better resistance to knocking**.

## Which Fuel Is Right for Your Car?

The answer is simpler than you'd expect. **Follow the manufacturer's recommendation** — that's it.

- **Cars that require premium:** High-performance, high-compression engines are designed with premium fuel in mind. Using regular gas can reduce performance or cause engine knock.
- **Cars that recommend regular:** The majority of everyday vehicles. Filling these with premium gives you **almost no noticeable benefit** — you're just paying more.

Check the sticker inside your fuel cap or your owner's manual for the recommended fuel. Once you know, you'll never have to wonder again.

## A Few Things to Keep in Mind

- **Don't downgrade from premium if your car requires it.** Consistently using regular in a premium-required vehicle can affect performance and long-term durability.
- **Watch out for suspiciously cheap "premium."** If a gas station's premium price seems too good to be true, stick to reputable stations.
- **If you don't feel a difference, skip the premium.** For cars designed for regular, saving money is the smarter move.

## Conclusion

The premium vs. regular debate isn't about quality — it's about **compatibility with your car**. The most accurate and economical approach is simply to follow what the manufacturer recommends. If your car runs on regular, don't chase premium — chase **the best price on the fuel you actually need**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

**Q. Is it worth putting premium in a regular-gas car?**
For regular-recommended vehicles, you'll feel almost no difference — you're just paying more. There's no real reason to switch.

**Q. Can I use regular gas in a car that requires premium?**
We don't recommend it. Performance can suffer, and engine knock may occur. Use the recommended fuel whenever possible.

**Q. How do I find out what fuel my car needs?**
Check the sticker inside your fuel filler cap or your vehicle's owner's manual. Once you know, you'll never have to guess again.

**Q. Does premium keep my engine cleaner?**
Regular gas also contains detergent additives. Switching to premium in a regular-gas car won't produce a noticeable improvement in engine cleanliness.

**Q. Does a higher octane rating mean more power?**
No. Octane measures knock resistance, not power output. You won't feel a performance boost unless your car is specifically designed for premium.

**Q. Does occasionally mixing in premium help?**
If regular is what's recommended, there's no meaningful benefit to expect. That money is better spent finding the cheapest regular station.

**Q. Is premium fuel economy actually better?**
In vehicles designed for premium, there can be a case for it. In regular-gas cars, the savings from better economy rarely offset the higher price.

**Q. What's the most economical fueling strategy overall?**
Fill up with your manufacturer's recommended fuel, at the cheapest nearby station, using a fuel discount card. That combination wins every time.
